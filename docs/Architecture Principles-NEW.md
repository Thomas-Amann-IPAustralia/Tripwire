# Architecture Principles

### Introduction

Architecture principles are part of the Enterprise Architecture capability and contribute to the alignment of solutions with business goals and strategies. 



A principle is: “a basic generalisation that is accepted as true and that can be used as a basis for reasoning.”



The Enterprise Architecture principles form the criteria against which all potential investment and architectural decisions are made.



All business information solution initiatives should begin with an examination for compliance with the principles. If, after consultation, it is decided to implement a solution in spite of a conflict with a principle (in order to meet some short term goal, for example), then the cost of realigning the implementation must be included in the costs of the solution and a plan for correcting the situation must be created. Continued focus on short term goals will negate the benefits of the enterprise architecture approach and result in increased long-term cost and risk.



These principles will be used to guide and shape the development of reference architectures, patterns and policies that will be documented in the domain architectures. These will exist at a more concrete level that can be more easily and directly applied within projects.

Statement:

Business Solutions\[1] must be aligned with business objectives, goals and strategies to deliver business outcomes effectively and efficiently.

\[1] Note that the term “business solution” includes all business capabilities and processes. It does not imply, nor preclude any ICT support.



Rationale:

* Business outcomes must drive solutions.
* Solutions should address business outcomes including those projected in enterprise strategic plans and annual operational plans.
* Solutions need to provide maximum benefit to the Australian community as a whole, as well as the Australian Government and Australian economy. Technology investments must be coordinated with \[REDACTED ORGANISATION]'s business in support of specific and measurable benefits to the organisation.  Solutions need to be efficient by cost effectively delivering outcomes.

### Ownership

Tagline:

\[REDACTED ORGANISATION] resources must have an owner\[2]

* standards

### Cloud First

Tagline:

Adopt; Adapt; Buy; Build



Statement:

Take a cloud-first approach to ensure efficient and effective delivery of business outcomes through use of commodity services and reuse of proven solutions.



Rationale:

* \[REDACTED ORGANISATION] has clearly stated its strategic direction to use cloud computing services for all new solutions, and to migrate existing solutions to cloud where practicable (with due consideration of risk and value for money).
* To reduce costs and deliver solutions to meet business outcomes quickly:
* direct re-use of existing capability within existing solutions or technologies is preferred;
* followed by the adaptation of existing solutions or technologies to meet specific requirements;
* followed by purchase of SaaS/hosted COTS (commercial-off-the-shelf) products;
* followed by commercial products that can be configured or used as components (e.g. use of PaaS solutions); and
* build bespoke solutions using IaaS only when other options are not viable or available (e.g. to address unique business requirements).
* When buying, integrated systems made up of components which work together may be better than an array of best of breed point solutions which are not integrated. The use of open standards, methods and technologies reduces vendor lock-in and allows for simpler and more reliable integration, interoperability and portability of solution components.
* With cloud computing services, the preference is to choose SaaS over PaaS over IaaS (see Cloud Decision Framework for details).

Statement:

* Benefits realisation claims that are supported with empirical measurements guarantee value is being created by the delivered outcomes, contributing to a results-driven culture.

### Asset

Tagline:

Information is managed as an asset



Statement:

Information \[REDACTED ORGANISATION] manages is a national asset, whose value can only be realised through sharing and re-use.



Rationale:

* \[REDACTED ORGANISATION] does not own the information it manages. We manage information under our control on behalf of the Australian Government.
* The information we manage is relied on for decision making. Decision makers need to know how fit-for-purpose the information they use is.

### Shared and Reused

Tagline:

Information is shared and reused



Statement:

Information is to be collected once and used for as many business operations as are required.



Rationale:

* Information increases in value the more it is used.
* Sharing and re-using information reduces the overheads of capturing and maintaining it.
* It provides motivation for managing the information according to standards and reduces data conflicts and ambiguity.
* Re-use of information internationally can benefit us and the IP system as a whole.
* This approach is consistent with the Data Matters strategy: The right data is available to those who need it – anywhere and anytime.
* This approach is consistent with the application of the Digital Service Standard.



### Available

Tagline:

Information is available when needed

Statement:

* Users of \[REDACTED ORGANISATION] information (internal and external) need access to the right information at the right time.
* The information needs to be timely and relevant.
* It needs to be accessible - it can be discovered and people/systems are able use it according to their role in the surety that the information is what they asked for.
* If a service cannot guarantee confidentiality, integrity and availability, people will not use it.



Rationale:

* The storage, discovery and access to accurate reliable information is a major input to our ongoing business.
* \[REDACTED ORGANISATION] has various legislative, Whole of Government and international obligations to make its information available in human-readable and machine-readable forms that conform to relevant standards.
* The ability to find relevant information is key to achieving business efficiency and satisfying stakeholder needs.
* Finding information is composed of two components: discoverability - being able to find out the information exists; and accessibility - being able to get the information. Getting the information requires a usable information service and an access control environment that grants appropriate access to classified information.



### Single Authoritative Source

Tagline:

Information is managed with a single authoritative source of truth

Statement:

* Information items must have a single identified authoritative source.
* Information must be sourced from this authoritative source.
* If a service cannot guarantee confidentiality, integrity and availability, people will not use it.



Rationale:

* Providing a single authoritative source of truth for an information item is the first step to ensuring the accuracy and consistency of information.
* As updates are carried out in the authoritative source, not on copies, we can determine the current state of a data item with more assurance.

### Granular and Loosely Coupled

Tagline:

Business solution components to be Granular and Loosely coupled.



Statement:

Promote:

* Components to be aligned with business services; Critical thinking in achieving short term gains whilst considering long term goals;
* Deeper consideration for the alignment of business outcomes with underpinning ICT architecture;

### Manage Diversity

Tagline:

Technology diversity to be managed



Statement:

Technology diversity must be controlled minimise total cost of ownership (TCO), while retaining the ability to innovate and take advantage of new and emerging services available in the cloud and shared service ecosystems.



Rationale:

Diversity of computing platforms needs to be reduced to achieve a simpler, more manageable and cost-effective technology environment. Diversity in this principle relates to configuration as well as hardware and software to allow automated, standardised and reproducible processes and procedures to be created and used. This approach will minimise the number of variations of technology instances within \[REDACTED ORGANISATION]'s technology environment, allowing the maximum value to be realised from each technology choice, and in turn reducing technology TCO. The strategic direction to use cloud computing services will introduce new technologies, but much of the underlying complexity is managed by the service provider, and cost is closely aligned to actual usage.

### Manageable and Robust

Tagline:

Technology to be manageable and robust.



Statement:

Technology used by \[REDACTED ORGANISATION] must be able to be monitored, supported, administered and maintained, and must provide the level of robustness required and agreed by \[REDACTED ORGANISATION]. \[REDACTED ORGANISATION] solutions must be architected to take advantage of the inherent resilience available in cloud computing to satisfy high availability and disaster recovery requirements.



Rationale:

* Technology solutions must be architected and built to be managed. This includes the ability to monitor and report on performance and availability as well as the ability to administer technology through standard and sustainable processes. Additionally, technology should be maintainable without causing undue impact on business operations. The level of maintainability and the degree to which technology maintenance impacts on business operations will be largely determined by the level of robustness required by \[REDACTED ORGANISATION]. Robust systems are designed to be available when users need to access them, and to minimise the operational impacts of unplanned and planned service outages. There is a direct correlation between levels of robustness and cost – the more available the system needs to be, the more expensive the system will cost throughout its life. The use of modern just-in-time cloud services to achieve availability and reliability can reduce this cost.
* The technology you choose to build your service must help you respond quickly and regularly to the needs and expectations of users, and to successfully and continuously deliver the digital service. Another requirement is to have a procurement approach that will not restrict, inhibit or limit ongoing and future service delivery.
* Note: This principle intentionally avoids the use of specific industry terms such as "Fault Tolerant" and “High Availability’ to avoid any specific interpretation of robustness.
